# Competitive Landscape: Unitree Skill Store

## Why Unitree matters

In December 2025, Unitree publicized what it describes as a humanoid robot app/skill store. Reporting describes an Action Library where users and developers can upload, share, and download actions or datasets, with examples such as martial arts, dance, ballet, and other motion routines.

Public reports also describe automatic sync into a mobile app/Applet Library and a Datasets section for training data. The most important implication is market validation: humanoid robot capabilities are beginning to look like downloadable assets.

## ETD positioning

Unitree appears to be pushing a consumer/developer action marketplace. ETD should position itself as an **industrial skill-runtime and validation framework**:

- stricter manifests;
- capability policy;
- station compatibility;
- safety boundaries;
- failure simulation;
- validation reports;
- release packages;
- enterprise event integration.

## Difference

| Dimension | Unitree-style store | ETD prototype |
|---|---|---|
| Primary focus | Actions, datasets, applets | Validated industrial skill packages |
| Early examples | Dance, martial arts, demonstrations | Pick/place, assembly, inspection, cobot handover |
| Safety model | Not fully visible publicly | Explicit forbidden capabilities and bounded skill intents |
| Enterprise readiness | Emerging | Designed for station profiles, reports, adapters |
| Runtime role | Platform-specific | Platform-agnostic application-layer standard |

## Strategic conclusion

Unitree validates the direction. ETD should not compete as another entertainment action store. It should compete as the safety-aware packaging, validation, and deployment layer for industrial humanoid skills.
